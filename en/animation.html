Stickfight Animation

A 45-second animated fight scene, from concept to delivery — built with mask path tracing, hand-drawn frames, and heavy reference study in After Effects.

Role
Animator
Tool
Adobe After Effects
Length
45 seconds
Framerate
24 fps

Introduction

This is the process behind my animation, from concept to delivery: a 45-second fight scene, supported by choreography and a storyboard, a set of references, and a mix of tools and techniques.

Tools

The animation was made in After Effects — a tool capable of a lot more than people usually give it credit for. I started with mask path tracing: drawing simple, stick-like figures and animating them by moving their joints (the mask points) and keyframing every frame. After Effects redraws the shape between those moved points automatically, which made posing fast to iterate on.

For the setting, I built a grid-textured environment in a 3D space — a technique commonly used by beginner fight-scene animators to give a sense of depth without spending excessive time on a full background. On top of that I used a camera component, which let me animate camera movement without redrawing the background every frame, making the fight feel a lot more immersive in the process.

With those systems in place, I eventually switched over to frame-by-frame hand-drawn animation to capture the sequence more precisely than the rigged approach allowed. Finally, I used After Effects' effects library for post-processing — motion blur, gaussian blur, glow, and color balancing — to pull the whole thing together.

Storyboard & choreography

I wanted to carry the full length of the animation through combat alone. No words are exchanged, but the choreography still tells a story: Red is strong, with major endurance, relying on pure brawn. Blue is fast and technical, working through an arsenal of weapons and relying on pure technique. The moral of the fight is "cockiness leads to defeat." I deliberately gave Blue a long, flashy sequence to make the fight feel one-sided — only for him to get cocky, and get defeated by a single devastating blow.

Techniques

I leaned on a range of animation techniques common to the action/combat genre, animating on 24fps. I focused purely on a smooth, eye-candy sequence, sacrificing background and sound design in the process due to time constraints — the goal was smooth combat, above everything else.

Each character got his own set of techniques to make his fighting style pop:

  • Red gets impact frames and camera shake, which really sell him as a heavy-hitter. His final blow uses a tension hold / frame-stop — delaying the explosion creates a sense of anticipation and amplifies the final impact.
  • Blue gets smears and dynamic camera movement, which make him seem too fast for the eye to catch — sometimes turning him into a single white line to imply that speed.

References

I pulled a lot of references from combat-driven anime (sakuga) in particular, and studied realistic footage of people to nail the "follow-through" of throwing motions.

  • Knife tossing — Naruto Shippuden
  • Explosion — Soul Punisher, from Dragon Ball Super: Broly
  • Heel strike — "I M P A C T F R A M E S" on YouTube
  • Demonstration of speed — "Flying Raijin", from Naruto
  • Tumble ragdoll — Naruto vs Sasuke, Valley of the End
  • Energy-ball follow-through — footage of baseball pitching
